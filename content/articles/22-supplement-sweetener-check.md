*Written by the Natural Supplements Singapore editorial team · Last updated: 23 August 2026*

A supplement sweetener check helps a Singapore buyer identify what makes a chewable, gummy, powder, or effervescent product taste sweet without turning one ingredient into a verdict on the whole product. This educational guide, not medical advice, shows how to record the sweetener name, stated function, serving basis, warnings, and surrounding ingredient context.

## Start with the label, not the front claim

HSA's [health-supplement overview](https://www.hsa.gov.sg/health-supplements/overview/) says product information in English should be adequate and truthful. Its [labelling standards](https://file.go.gov.sg/hsa-chp-labelling-guidelines.pdf) distinguish outer, inner, small-container, blister, and leaflet information, and set out core items such as active ingredients, directions, warnings, storage, and pack size.

A sweetener is normally part of the wider formulation rather than the active-ingredient amount being promoted on the front. Look for terms such as “sweetener,” “humectant,” or “bulking agent” in the full ingredient list. Do not infer absence from “natural,” “clean,” “no added sugar,” or a low sugar value; each phrase answers a different question.

HSA also links Singapore users to the ASEAN principles for additives and excipients. The accompanying [HSA industry FAQ](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/409/3bf03c4d-8348-4279-b90a-eaba3a114d7f/guidance_faq_add_excp.pdf) explains an official lookup route for whether a specific additive or excipient appears for food supplements under stated conditions. That is a compliance-research route, not a personal suitability test.

## Build a seven-field sweetener record

This first original element converts the official labelling and excipient routes into a repeatable record, captured on 23 August 2026.

| Field | What to copy | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Product and dosage form | Exact name; gummy, chewable, powder, liquid, or effervescent tablet | Keeps comparisons like-for-like |
| Sweetener name | The label wording, including group terms | Avoids treating all sweeteners as one ingredient |
| Stated function | Sweetener, humectant, bulking agent, or another disclosed role | Separates label role from assumption |
| Position and companions | Nearby acids, flavours, colours, coatings, or sugars | Preserves formulation context |
| Serving basis | Ingredients and any quantities per unit or stated serving | Prevents pack-level and serving-level confusion |
| Warnings and suitability | Only the wording actually shown | Flags information needing professional discussion |
| Version evidence | Page or pack URL, capture date, batch or expiry when available | Records which evidence was checked |

Write “not stated on the checked page” when a field is absent. Do not convert an ingredient-list position into a quantity, or assume that the same named sweetener is used at the same level across products.

## Use a seven-step label check

1. Photograph or save the exact pack panel or public product page and record the capture date.
2. Identify the dosage form before comparing products; a gummy and a capsule solve different formulation problems.
3. Copy every sweetener name exactly, including grouped wording and spelling variants.
4. Record the function stated beside each ingredient instead of assigning one from memory.
5. Read the surrounding ingredient list for sugars, flavours, acids, coatings, colours, and bulking agents.
6. Match any quantity or warning to its stated unit, serving, and maximum daily basis.
7. Keep the sweetener record separate from active-ingredient amounts, health claims, and individual-use decisions.

This check is designed to improve traceability. It cannot determine tolerance, an interaction, or whether a product is appropriate for a particular person.

## FAQ

**Does “no added sugar” mean no sweeteners?**  
Not necessarily. Check the full ingredient list and any nutrition or warning panel. Do not treat a front phrase as a substitute for the formulation details.

**Are sorbitol and sucralose the same thing?**  
No. They are different named ingredients. Also record the role stated on the particular label rather than assuming a universal function from the name alone.

**Can ingredient order reveal the amount?**  
The checked HSA sources do not support using list position as a numerical amount. Record a quantity only when the label or attributable product evidence states it.

**Does a sweetener check show which product is healthier?**  
No. It documents label evidence. A health comparison would also require verified context about the whole formulation, intended use, serving basis, and the individual.

## Keep the conclusion narrow

A supplement sweetener check can make a label comparison more precise, but it cannot establish product quality, effectiveness, authenticity, or personal suitability.
