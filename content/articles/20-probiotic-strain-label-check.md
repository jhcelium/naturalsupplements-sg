*Written by the Natural Supplements Singapore editorial team · Last updated: 3 August 2026*

A probiotic strain label check helps Singapore supplement buyers separate a broad species name from the fuller identity and quantity printed for each microorganism. This educational guide, not medical advice, shows how to record strain codes, CFU amounts, serving basis, storage details, and source dates without treating a large number or a longer strain list as proof of effectiveness or personal suitability.

## What a strain-level name adds

The January 2026 [HSA Health Supplements Guidelines](https://file.go.gov.sg/hsa-hs-guidelines.pdf) include probiotics among the substances that can fall within the health-supplement category. HSA's September 2023 [labelling standards](https://file.go.gov.sg/hsa-chp-labelling-guidelines.pdf) recommend internationally accepted nomenclature for active-ingredient names and define quantity as the amount of each active ingredient in each dosage form or recommended dose.

Those rules provide a Singapore label-reading base, but product pages can split the name, code, CFU amount, and serving basis across sections. Reunite those fields before comparing products.

A 2019 [PLOS ONE study indexed by PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30901338/) reported strain-level diversity among commercial isolates carrying the same species name. It did not test either product below. The narrow label lesson is that species and strain designation are different identity fields.

## Build a six-field strain record

This first original element combines the verified HSA labelling fields with peer-reviewed strain context, captured on 3 August 2026.

| Field | What to copy | Safe interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Genus and species | The full microorganism name | Identifies the taxonomic name shown on the source |
| Strain designation | The letters, numbers, or code in brackets | Distinguishes the named strain record from species-only wording |
| CFU amount | The number and unit for that strain | Records the quantity basis displayed by the source |
| Serving basis | Per capsule, tablet, serving, or daily dose | Prevents comparison of unlike units |
| Storage instruction | Temperature and dry, light, or refrigeration wording | Preserves the conditions stated for the product |
| Source and date | Pack, official page, URL, and capture date | Shows which version of the information was checked |

Write “not shown on checked source” when a field is missing. Do not infer a strain code from a brand name, add separate strains together without checking the serving basis, or convert CFU into a quality or effectiveness score.

## Use a seven-step label checklist

1. Copy the full genus and species exactly as displayed; do not shorten the record too early.
2. Record the strain designation beside the correct species rather than in a separate unlabelled note.
3. Copy each CFU amount and confirm whether it is per capsule, tablet, serving, or daily dose.
4. Add the strain rows and compare the sum with any front-page total; note discrepancies instead of correcting the source yourself.
5. Record the dosage form, directions, storage temperature, and dry, light, or refrigeration wording.
6. Compare the current official page with the physical pack when available, because page copy and packaging can change.
7. Save the URL and capture date, and refer medicine, pregnancy, child-use, allergy, reaction, or suitability questions to a qualified doctor or pharmacist.

This checklist is an evidence record, not a dosing method or a substitute for professional advice.

## FAQ

**Is a species name the same as a strain designation?**  
No. The species is the broader taxonomic name; the added letters or numbers identify the strain record shown on the label. Keep both when the source provides them.

**Does a higher CFU number mean a better probiotic?**  
Not by itself. The displayed number must be read with the named strain, serving basis, storage instructions, expiry information, and evidence relevant to the exact product. This article does not set an ideal CFU amount.

**Can I compare two headline totals directly?**  
Only after checking whether both totals use the same unit and basis. One page may state CFU per capsule while another uses a multi-unit daily dose. Reconcile the individual rows before comparing totals.

**Does “fridge-free” mean storage conditions do not matter?**  
No. Follow the current product's printed temperature, moisture, light, seal, and expiry instructions. “Fridge-free” does not mean storage-free.

## Keep the check within its limits

A probiotic strain label check improves traceability, but it cannot verify laboratory identity, viability through expiry, effectiveness, interactions, or individual suitability. This article is general consumer education, not medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, prevention, dosing guidance, or a recommendation to use or stop a supplement. If you take medicines, are pregnant or nursing, are buying for a child, have a health condition, or are concerned about a reaction, speak with a qualified doctor or pharmacist.
